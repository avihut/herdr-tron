# Research and design decisions

Research date: 2026-11-07. Sources were read directly; production artwork was visually inspected in the browser. No reference frames, film artwork, official logos or soundtrack files are redistributed.

## Primary references

- [GMUNK — TRON Opening Titles](https://gmunk.com/TRON-Opening-Titles): production account and original imagery. The team used an architectural grid and developed luminous paths into spatial city forms; the director preferred a simpler result over excessive detail. Visually inspected the montage of thin cyan wireframe paths, elevated structures and deep black negative space. Adaptation: branching light-path buses, luminous status discs, manual spatial geometry and generous terminal area. The user requested stronger neon circuitry after the first version: recognizable source motifs must remain visible even when the terminal is the main surface.
- [Digital Domain — TRON: Legacy](https://digitaldomain.com/work/tron-legacy/): identifies the production's environments, light cycles, Recognizers, characters and discs as stereoscopic CG work. Adaptation: original bounded 3D disc, not a claim to reproduce production models.
- [Phil Saunders — TRON: Legacy keyframes](https://philsaunders.artstation.com/projects/okwWL): artist's explanation of lighting/mood keyframes over art-department models and design contributors. Read for production context; imagery was not extracted or bundled.
- [Walt Disney Archives — TRON](https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/news/tron-archives/): original costume, helmet and identity-disc artifacts. Grounds the useful disc metaphor without copying an official prop model.
- [D23 — TRON (film)](https://d23.com/a-to-z/tron-film/): grounds the 1982 ENCOM/Flynn archive reference and the original computer-world premise.
- [Disney Parks — World of TRON](https://disneyparksblog.com/disney-experiences/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-world-of-tron/): official introduction to Programs, Users, the Grid, identity discs and light cycles.
- [Alvy Ray Smith — A Biography of the Pixel annotations](https://alvyray.com/DigitalLight/ANNOTATIONS_DigitalLight_v29Jun23.pdf): includes a correction describing Bit's yellow YES/red NO forms. TRON uses readable binary words rather than claiming an exact character reconstruction.

## Canon versus adaptation

The Grid, Programs, Users, ENCOM, identity discs, light cycles, Bit and the phrase “End of line” are source-world references. Herdr sectors/routes, attention queues, the four destination screens, puzzle rules, cosmetic circuit colors and achievement conditions are original adaptations. The portal map is deliberately a real system directory, not invented geography. The ISO-inspired reward is a brief thematic discovery, not a simulated entity or a claim about the user's agents.

The primary direction is Legacy's black surfaces, pale luminous edges and measured geometry. Light chrome and violet classic-grid circuitry are accessibility/cosmetic adaptations, not frame-faithful reproductions. Geist is Herdr's available readable UI typeface; no trademark logotype or decorative alphabet replaces essential text.

Native geometry keeps the art portable. The disc is an original annular solid with 24 segments and eight surfaces per segment. Rotation changes XYZ coordinates before lighting/projection; 192 faces bound its render cost. A native opacity loop indicates actual working status. Everything stops moving under reduced motion except explicit user actions.
